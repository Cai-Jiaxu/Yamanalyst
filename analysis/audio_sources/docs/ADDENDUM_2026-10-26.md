## 1. Tonal whistle detector (new, measured)

Script: `bin/extract_whistle_tonal.py`.
Method: STFT, 1024-window @ 16 kHz (93.7 Hz bins), 10 ms hop. For each hop
find the peak bin in 2-5 kHz; compute spectral concentration
(peak_energy / band_energy) and frequency stability (std of peak bin across
4 hops = 40 ms frame). Combined `whistle_score = conc * stability * loud`.

Runtime: 13.5 s decode + 16.6 s STFT = **30.5 s total** on CPU for the full
match. Cache: `cache/whistle_tonal.npz` (199,315 frames x 5 float32 columns).

Event count vs threshold (events >= 160 ms):

| threshold | events |
|---:|---:|
| 0.20 | 790 |
| 0.25 | 415 |
| 0.30 | 206 |
| 0.35 | 96 |
| 0.40 | 45 |
| 0.45 | 12 |
| 0.50 | 3 |
| 0.60 | 0 |

Label-free alignment to known kickoff frames (at thr=0.30):

| known event | frame | nearest event mid | offset |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1H kickoff | 11185 | 11152 | **-1.32 s** |
| 2H kickoff | 113742 | 113708 | **-1.36 s** |
| 2H end | 190197 | 190216 | **+0.76 s** |

Interpretation: the tonal detector DOES fire within ~1.5 s of the three
known time boundaries. The naive band-energy prototype missed these.
Events at thr=0.3 (206 across 106 min = ~1.9/min) include the kickoff
whistles plus unknown other tonal sounds (crowd chants, vuvuzelas,
referee whistle echoes, ball impacts). Without hand labels we cannot
report precision/recall; we report detector counts and known-boundary
offsets only.

**Correction to earlier note**: I previously wrote "~50-100 referee
whistles per match" as an expectation. That was unsourced and unmeasured.
Retracted. The measured fact is: 206 tonal events at thr=0.3, of which
at least 3 land within 1.5 s of known kickoff/end boundaries. The true
whistle count is unknown without labels.

## 4. External audiovisual corpora (new research)

| corpus | what | audio? | per-frame possession labels? | license / access | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SoccerNet v2** | 550 broadcast games, ~300k event annotations (action spotting: goal, corner, card, sub, etc.) | yes, broadcast | **no**, event timestamps only | gated; some NDA; free subset on HuggingFace | free with account; large download |
| **SoccerNet-Echoes** | commentary ASR transcriptions on SoccerNet video | yes (commentary stream) | no | GitHub https://github.com/SoccerNet/sn-echoes | free |
| **IDSSE / Sportec Open DFL** | 7 Bundesliga matches, tracking + events, per-frame BallPossession/BallStatus | **no audio in the released bundle** | yes (already our training set) | CC BY 4.0 | free |
| **Metrica sample games** | 2 games, tracking + broadcast video | unknown (need to check the sample mp4) | yes (already used) | sample license | free |
| **RoboCup whistle-2017** | labelled whistle clips, robot-soccer mic | yes | whistle labels only | research use | small, free |
| **AudioSet / YAMNet classes** | 521 generic sound classes incl. Whistle, Crowd, Cheering | n/a (pretrained model) | n/a (generic event detection) | YAMNet: Apache 2.0; PANNs: research | free local inference |

Key caveat (parent flagged): external training matches have NO audio, so
an all-null audio column on ext cannot learn splits. The `audio_present=0`
trick does not solve this because the tree sees no positive audio examples
at all. Feasible paths:

(a) **Representation extraction only**: run YAMNet/PANNs on our match,
treat the output as a per-frame feature. Do NOT expect ext yardstick to
move; evaluate on masked/public/val_h2 only. Accept that the ext fold
will see NaN and the model will learn to ignore audio there.

(b) **Self-supervised pretraining**: train a small audio encoder on our
match's unlabelled audio (contrastive / masked reconstruction), then use
it as a frozen feature extractor. This does not need labels and does not
need external audio. Risk: our match's audio distribution is narrow
(one stadium, one broadcast), so the encoder may not generalize.

(c) **SoccerNet audio as auxiliary pretraining**: download a small subset
of SoccerNet broadcast audio, train a whistle/crowd classifier on their
event timestamps, then run it on our audio. This uses external audio but
not external possession labels. Eligibility: SoccerNet has an NDA for
some content; the free subset is downloadable. Cost: hours of download +
inference.

## 7. PANNs Cnn14 local pilot (new, measured)

Script: `bin/panns_pilot.py`. Log: `logs/panns_pilot.log`.
Cache: `cache/panns_kickoff_window.npz` + `cache/panns_kickoff_window_summary.json`.

Weights: Cnn14 trained on AudioSet (mAP=0.431), 327 MB .pth downloaded from
Zenodo (https://zenodo.org/record/3987831/files/Cnn14_mAP%3D0.431.pth?download=1).
License: PANNs code is MIT, weights are AudioSet-derived (research use).
Local-only inference on CPU. No match media uploaded.

Setup:
- `uv pip install --target=pylibs panns_inference librosa torchlibrosa` (isolated).
- Downloaded `class_labels_indices.csv` from storage.googleapis.com.
- Downloaded weights to `/Users/bytedance/panns_data/Cnn14_mAP=0.431.pth`.

Runtime on this machine (Apple Silicon, CPU):
- Model load: 20.9 s.
- Inference: 44.4 ms per 1.0 s chunk.
- 60 s window around 1H kickoff ran end-to-end in 27.5 s.
- **Full-match extrapolation**: 7972 s / 1 s chunks x 44 ms = ~353 s =
  ~6 minutes CPU. Feasible as a one-off offline extraction.

Results around 1H kickoff (frame 11185, t=447.4 s):

| class | idx | peak chunk | t_rel to kickoff | peak p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Crowd | 69 | 0 | -29.5 s (pregame) | 0.187 |
| Applause | 67 | 52 | +22.5 s | 0.135 |
| Cheering | 66 | 20 | -9.5 s | 0.080 |
| Whoop | 10 | 20 | -9.5 s | 0.063 |
| Yell | 11 | 20 | -9.5 s | 0.015 |
| Shout | 8 | 20 | -9.5 s | 0.009 |
| **Buzzer** | **398** | **28** | **-1.5 s** | **0.003** |
| **Whistle** | **402** | **28** | **-1.5 s** | **0.002** |

Comparison vs tonal detector:
- Tonal detector fired at frame 11152 = -1.32 s from kickoff.
- PANNs Whistle and Buzzer both peak at chunk 28 = -1.5 s from kickoff.
- **Exploratory only**: in this single selected window, the two methods
  peak within 0.2 s of each other. This is NOT event-accuracy evidence.
  Whistle p=0.002 and Buzzer p=0.003 are near the floor of the model's
  output; they do not independently confirm a whistle. We selected this
  window because we already knew a kickoff occurred there (selection
  bias). No precision/recall is reported.
- Crowd/Cheering/Applause scores (p=0.05-0.19) are larger in magnitude
  and track the expected pregame envelope. Whether they are reliable
  possession signals is untested.

Interpretation (strict measured vs hypothesis):
- **Measured**: generic pretrained weights load and run locally on CPU at
  44 ms/chunk. Full-match cost extrapolates to ~6 min but has NOT been
  measured end-to-end.
- **Measured**: in one hand-picked kickoff window, PANNs Whistle and
  Buzzer probabilities are ~0.002-0.003, near output floor.
- **Hypothesis (not proven)**: the low Whistle probability is due to
  1 s chunk dilution. We did not test a shorter chunk or frame-level model.
- **Hypothesis (not proven)**: Cnn14_DecisionLevelMax would sharpen the
  Whistle score. Not run.
- **Hypothesis (not proven)**: Crowd/Cheering/Applause scores would be
  useful possession features. No classification eval done.
- **NOT a substitute for classification evaluation**: these are raw model
  outputs, not labels. Running them as features requires downstream eval
  against the bench.

Key point: external audiovisual corpora do NOT provide possession labels.
They can provide (a) auxiliary audio pretraining (SoccerNet whistle/crowd
classifier) or (b) generic event scores (PANNs/YAMNet) as direct features.
They cannot close the "no audio in external training matches" gap.
